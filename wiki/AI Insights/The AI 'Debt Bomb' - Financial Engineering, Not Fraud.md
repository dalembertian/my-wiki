# The AI 'Debt Bomb': Financial Engineering, Not Fraud

An accountant's rebuttal to the "AI debt bomb" narrative, arguing the underlying financing structure is well-precedented and the risk is real but not fraud-shaped.

## The mechanism

Meta, Oracle, xAI, CoreWeave and others fund datacenter buildouts through **special-purpose vehicles**: a separate, non-consolidated entity raises debt from outside investors/banks (some from the parent itself), builds the datacenter, and grants the parent exclusive use once complete — keeping most of the debt off the parent's own balance sheet. The Financial Times reported over $120bn shifted off-balance-sheet this way by Dec 2025; Goldman Sachs projects hyperscalers could spend $5.3tn on AI/datacenters through 2030, increasingly financed by private markets.

## Why "Enron 2.0" is the wrong comparison

Enron's collapse was caused by fraud — hiding losses, not just moving debt off the books. The author draws instead on personal experience: in the 1980s-90s, biotech firms (Centocor and peers like Genentech, Amgen, Biogen) routinely funded drug development through similar off-balance-sheet limited partnerships. Individual drug failures didn't cause market panics, because the risk was intentionally spread across investors who understood and accepted it. The structure is old; only the asset class (AI infrastructure vs. drug candidates) changed.

Two things make today's version *more* recoverable than the biotech precedent, not less:

- **Disclosure and scrutiny are far higher now** — pre-internet, pre-SEC-disclosure biotech deals happened with little public visibility; today's SPVs face intense scrutiny and required disclosures.
- **The underlying asset doesn't disappear on failure.** A failed clinical trial produces nothing salvageable. A datacenter, even an underperforming one, is land, buildings, electrical infrastructure, and compute — sellable or repurposable. Jeff Bezos's framing: AI is an "industrial bubble," the kind that leaves behind useful infrastructure (railways, fiber-optic cable) even when the bubble itself bursts.

## Is there even a glut?

The data cited argues no, not yet: North American datacenter capacity grew 36% last year and vacancy still fell to a record 1.4% — demand is outpacing supply in nearly every major market (CBRE). Microsoft estimates only 17.8% of the world's working-age population currently uses generative AI, suggesting adoption is still early rather than plateauing.

## The actual risk, stated plainly

Some of these investments will fail; some lenders will lose money; some datacenters will be worth less than their owners paid. That's the explicit purpose of the financing structure — spreading large capital risk among investors willing to take it, with obligations disclosed and assets real. The author's conclusion: "I see financial engineering, yes. I don't see a debt bomb."
