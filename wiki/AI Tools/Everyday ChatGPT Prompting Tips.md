# Everyday ChatGPT Prompting Tips

A practitioner's cookbook of everyday ChatGPT prompts, merged from two similar listicles (Tom's Guide). The theoretical companion to this is [[Prompt Engineering - Is It a New Programming Language]] — that page covers the "is this a discipline" debate; this one is the day-to-day practitioner version.

## Shape the output

- **Specify the audience/reading level**: "explain X to a 10-year-old" vs. to an expert changes vocabulary, examples, and depth dramatically — often the single highest-leverage tweak.
- **Constrain format and length**: ask for tables/CSV instead of paragraphs for anything comparison-shaped; set hard limits ("exactly 3 paragraphs," "under 100 words") to force prioritization.
- **Request a specific voice or persona**: "in the style of Carl Sagan," or opening with "Imagine you're..." to fully commit the model to a persona rather than a generic neutral tone.

## Feed it context instead of doing the work yourself

- **Attach/paste documents for analysis** rather than reading them yourself — ask explicitly for key points, simplified language, and patterns, not just "summarize."
- **Give 2-3 examples before asking for more** (few-shot) — works especially well for classification, formatting, or matching a stylistic pattern, and removes ambiguity that a pure description leaves open.

## Use it as connective tissue for a decision

- **Weigh pros/cons of two options against stated priorities** — useful specifically when you want the emotion taken out of a decision.
- **Request feedback from a specific professional perspective** ("review as a marketing expert would," "as a university professor would") rather than generic feedback.
- **Simulate an interview or a difficult conversation** and ask for feedback on your answers — a low-stakes way to rehearse before the real thing.

## Everyday-life prompts

- Fact-check a viral claim/news story/image using search + deep research.
- Turn a fridge/pantry inventory into concrete dinner ideas.
- Gamify a boring task ("turn cleaning the garage into a mini challenge").
- Ask "tell me something about myself you think I don't know" (or "what's my most toxic trait") for reflective self-insight, if you're a regular/long-history user.
- Turn a raw task list into a prioritized, time-estimated, scheduled plan.

## Meta-prompting

Ask ChatGPT to draft a well-structured prompt **for a different AI tool** (Midjourney, Claude) rather than writing that prompt yourself — offloading prompt-craft to the model you're already fluent with.
