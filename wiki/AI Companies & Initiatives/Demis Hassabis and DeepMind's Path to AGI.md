# Demis Hassabis and DeepMind's Path to AGI

Demis Hassabis, a former chess prodigy and games programmer (he coded *Theme Park* at 17), co-founded DeepMind in 2010 with Shane Legg and Mustafa Suleyman on the mission "solve intelligence and then use it to solve everything else." Early investors included Peter Thiel and Elon Musk — both of whom pivoted to backing rival OpenAI instead after Google acquired DeepMind for £400m in 2014. Hassabis insisted on keeping DeepMind headquartered in London rather than moving to Silicon Valley.

## Track record

- **2014**: an AI mastering Atari games from scratch drew Silicon Valley's attention.
- **2016**: AlphaGo defeated Go grandmaster Lee Sedol — Go being far more complex than chess.
- **AlphaFold**: solved the structures of 200M+ proteins, made freely public — the basis of Hassabis's 2024 Nobel Prize in Chemistry.
- Notably, DeepMind was caught off guard by ChatGPT's 2022 release despite having comparable underlying systems: "We could see the flaws in it, like it would hallucinate... I don't think anyone fully understood, including OpenAI, that there would be these amazing use cases." His read: "an interesting lesson for us about how you can be a bit too close to your own technology."

Hassabis calls himself a reluctant public-facing AI ambassador — he'd have preferred to "leave it in the lab longer" and produce more AlphaFold-style breakthroughs before facing public scrutiny.

Hassabis expects something reasonably called AGI (matching all human cognitive capabilities) within **5-10 years**, possibly the lower end — see [[Free Intelligence and Radical Abundance]] for his post-AGI "radical abundance" argument, merged there with Bill Gates's near-identical "free intelligence" thesis since the two make essentially the same claim.

## Cross-reference

Hassabis's own "too close to the technology" admission about missing ChatGPT's impact is a rare instance of an industry optimist acknowledging the exact blind spot critics like [[Cory Doctorow - The Reverse-Centaur Critique of AI]] accuse the industry of.


[[The AI Revolution - From ANI to Superintelligence]] lays out the ANI→AGI→ASI framework Hassabis's timeline is stated within, and [[Inherent Labs and Faraday]] is a DeepMind-alumni spinout pursuing the research-automation piece of the same agenda.
