# Mews's AI Agents for Hotel Front Desks

Mews (hospitality property-management software) is beta-testing two front-of-house AI agents, explicitly designed to keep humans making the final call.

- **Guest Action Assistant** turns existing guest-profile insights (VIP status, preferences) directly into one-click actionable tasks — welcome amenities, housekeeping flags, upgrade offers — instead of leaving staff to interpret a summary and manually create the task themselves. Framed as "insight + action = meaningful support."
- **Stay Allocation Assistant** simulates the downstream impact of early check-ins and late checkouts across room inventory and cleaning schedules, surfacing recommendations: which room to use, when a paid upgrade is viable, when a safer alternative is needed. Explicitly advisory — "the agent doesn't make decisions for staff... it advises, explains, and lets staff approve."

Positioned as an early testing ground to validate trust in AI recommendations before extending scope, with beta recruitment aimed at front desk teams, GMs, revenue leaders, and front office managers.

## Cross-reference

Notably modest in ambition next to frontier-lab agent announcements elsewhere in this wiki — a useful small-scale, human-in-the-loop counterpoint to the "agents replacing white-collar work" framing in [[AI Doomsday Scenario Rattles US Markets]] and [[Cory Doctorow - The Reverse-Centaur Critique of AI]]: this is explicitly designed as a **centaur** (an assistant augmenting a human decision), not a reverse-centaur.


[[Is the AI Boom Real]] uses this deployment as a ground-truth check — a working agent rollout that keeps humans in the loop by design.

[[Who Will Check the AI]] singles this deployment out as the one place where the fix for disappearing human judgment is built into the product: an agent that advises and lets staff approve keeps them exercising the judgment, not just signing off on it.
