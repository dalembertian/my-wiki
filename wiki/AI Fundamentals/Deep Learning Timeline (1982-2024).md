# Deep Learning Timeline (1982-2024)

A compact chronology of how deep learning went from an academic backwater to the dominant force in computing, told through the people who carried it: Hopfield, Hinton, LeCun, Fei-Fei Li, and the Google Brain team. Useful as a "when did what happen" reference; the deeper treatments of individual milestones live on the linked pages.

## The timeline

| Year | Milestone |
|---|---|
| 1982 | **John Hopfield** introduces the Hopfield Network — a recurrent net acting as associative memory, framing neural systems in terms of energy landscapes (physics meets computation). |
| 1986 | **Hinton, Rumelhart, and Williams** publish backpropagation, making multi-layer network training feasible. |
| 1990s | **Yann LeCun** pioneers Convolutional Neural Networks, laying the groundwork for modern computer vision. |
| 2006 | **Fei-Fei Li** begins building **ImageNet** — eventually 14M+ labeled images. |
| 2012 | **AlexNet** (Alex Krizhevsky, co-supervised by Hinton, with Ilya Sutskever) wins the ImageNet Challenge on GPUs, proving deep learning at scale. |
| 2015 | OpenAI is founded by Musk, Altman, Brockman and others. |
| 2017 | Google researchers publish **"Attention is All You Need"**, introducing the Transformer and enabling BERT and GPT. |
| 2022 | **ChatGPT** launches (Nov), bringing generative AI into the mainstream. |
| 2023 | Meta releases **LLaMA**, pushing back against closed frontier models. |
| 2024 | **Hinton and Hopfield receive the Nobel Prize in Physics** for foundational work on neural networks. |

## The through-line

The article's framing is that each stage unlocked the next rather than replacing it. Hopfield and Hinton's 1980s work established that networks could learn; LeCun's CNNs established what to learn *with* for vision; Li's ImageNet supplied the data volume the theory had always needed; GPUs supplied the compute. Only once all four were in place did AlexNet land — and only then did industry pour money in.

Post-AlexNet, the piece emphasizes two democratizing forces: **open-source frameworks** (TensorFlow, PyTorch) that put model-building within reach of hobbyists and academics alike, and later **open-weight models** (LLaMA) that pushed back on frontier capability being locked behind a handful of labs. The 2024 Nobel is presented as the field's formal arrival — recognition that what had been an unfashionable research program is now infrastructure.

One caveat worth noting: the source is a lightly-sourced Substack chronology that explicitly asks readers to fact-check it, and it does contain loose attributions (e.g. crediting Andrej Karpathy as supporting AlexNet, and describing the 2024 Nobel in slightly inconsistent terms in different sections). Treat it as a shape-of-the-field overview rather than a citable record — the detail pages below are better grounded.
