# The AI Boom in Charts (June 2026)

A data snapshot of the AI boom as of June 2026, published as SpaceX sought a $1.77tn valuation, Anthropic filed for an IPO, and OpenAI was expected to follow.

- **Stocks**: the S&P 500 is up ~80% over five years, driven almost entirely by the "Magnificent Seven" (Alphabet, Amazon, Apple, Meta, Microsoft, Nvidia, Tesla) — 41 AI-related stocks now account for nearly half the index's total value. An analyst warns this concentration plus a possible private-credit freeze echoes dotcom-bubble conditions.
- **Capex**: AI infrastructure spending is projected to go from $765bn (2026) to $1.6tn (2031) per Goldman Sachs, which itself flags that even modest delays in datacentre delivery would invite scrutiny of the demand assumptions the spending is based on.
- **Adoption**: enterprise AI usage rose from 33% (2023) to ~80% now (McKinsey); ChatGPT hit 1bn monthly active users, a record for any app. The open question is whether usage converts into full end-to-end workflow automation, which is what would actually justify the spend.
- **Claude vs. ChatGPT**: Claude Code went viral among developers in late 2025 and Anthropic's traffic has been growing faster than ChatGPT's and Gemini's since, spiking after the Pentagon named Anthropic a supply-chain risk in March. Kentik projects Claude could overtake ChatGPT's traffic by summer 2026 — part of why Anthropic may have an easier IPO path than OpenAI.
- **Rising token costs**: usage ("tokenmaxx") is climbing faster than affordability — one unnamed company reportedly spent $500m in a single month on Claude Code licences. The core AI-ROI bet (productivity gains outpacing the bill) is under real pressure if costs keep outrunning measurable output gains.
- **Datacentre build-out**: 23GW of capacity was under construction globally in 2025; JLL projects +100GW by 2030 (~1,200 datacentres, doubling current capacity) — contingent on grid capacity and financing that isn't guaranteed to materialize.
- **Capability growth**: per METR, model capability (measured as human-task-time-equivalent) has been doubling roughly every 4 months; Anthropic's Claude Mythos reaches a 50% success rate on tasks that take human experts 8 hours to 2 days. Despite this, there's no commensurate labor-market impact yet — adoption bottlenecks include how much of executive judgment or legally sensitive work can safely be delegated.
- **GDP dependency**: US GDP kept growing through 2025-26 (2.1% in 2025, 1.6% in Q1 2026) despite government/industry layoffs — but a Harvard economist calculates that AI/datacentre-related "information processing equipment & software" investment accounted for 92% of US GDP growth in H1 2025. Headline growth is disproportionately an AI-capex artifact.
