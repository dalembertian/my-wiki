# AI Doomsday Scenario Rattles US Markets

In February 2026, a viral Substack post from a boutique research firm — framed explicitly as "a scenario, not a prediction" — set out a speculative path from today to an AI-driven economic crash by 2028, and real US markets reacted to it anyway. The S&P fell over 1%, its software component hit lows not seen since the April 2025 "liberation day" tariffs, and stocks explicitly named in the piece (Uber, DoorDash, Mastercard, American Express) dropped 4-6% in a single session.

## The scenario's mechanism

1. **AI agents remove economic "friction."** Once agents are capable enough (the piece points to Claude Code and Codex as the trigger), consumers and businesses stop paying middlemen — SaaS tools, travel/estate agents, ride-share and delivery apps, card networks — because an agent can just do the task or route around the fee. Companies monetizing friction (Uber, DoorDash, Visa/Mastercard, SaaS vendors like Oracle) get squeezed or bypassed.
2. **Mass white-collar unemployment, with no re-employment escape valve.** Unlike past automation waves, there's no adjacent job category to redeploy into, because the AI that displaced the original role is also capable of the "manage the AI" role. Displaced workers fall into gig work, wages compress, consumer spending drops, and firms respond to weaker demand by investing in *more* AI rather than people — a loop with no natural brake.
3. **Ripples into credit and mortgages.** Private credit lenders had underwritten software-company buyouts (the piece cites Zendesk) on the assumption of stable SaaS revenue; that assumption breaks, triggering the largest private-credit software default on record. Separately, laid-off white-collar workers can't service mortgages taken out against income that no longer exists.
4. **Reinforcing downward spirals**, ending in a ~57%-of-S&P crash in late 2027 — one central banks can't fix, because the cause is a real-economy shift (labor devalued by AI), not tight financial conditions.
5. **"Ghost GDP" and social fracture.** AI firms keep making money and propping up headline GDP even as it stops circulating through households; the government's tax base (built on taxing human labor) shrinks just as it needs to transfer more to people. The scenario ends with an "Occupy Silicon Valley"-style movement blockading AI firms' offices.

## Reception

Analysts quoted are skeptical of the scenario playing out as described, but note the reaction itself is the story: a single widely-shared thought piece moved markets that had shrugged off wars, tariffs, and banking scares. One framed it less as a forecast and more as "a wake-up call that the economy already no longer resembles the one just a few years ago."
